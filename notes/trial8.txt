1. The Frequency: Reconstitution vs. Rebalancing

ETFs don't update randomly; they follow a strict legal prospectus. There are two "shocks" to the system you need to track:

    Reconstitution (Annual/Semi-Annual): This is the "Big Bang." The index provider (like S&P or Russell) decides which companies are kicked out and which are added. For the Russell 2000, this happens every June.

    Rebalancing (Quarterly): This is "Weight Surgery." The list of companies stays mostly the same, but the weights are adjusted to account for market cap changes. This usually happens on the third Friday of the last month of each quarter (March, June, September, December).

    Confidence Score Impact: Your Lasso regression will be most accurate in the middle of a quarter. During the "Rebalance Week," your residuals (errors) will spike because the ETF is trading to match the new weights.

2. Is it costly for them? (The "Friction" Factor)

Yes, it is extremely costly for them to update. This works in your favor because it makes the basket "sticky."

    Transaction Costs: If VTI (with over $1 Trillion AUM) wants to change a weight by even 0.01%, they have to trade hundreds of millions of dollars.

    Market Impact: If they move too fast, they drive the price against themselves (Slippage).

    The "Sameness" Rule: Most ETFs aim for Low Turnover. They hate changing the basket because every trade eats into their "tracking error" and performance.
